## 2. ChunkFormer — what it is

- **Owner/organisation:** Author-maintained repository `khanld/chunkformer`; authors Khanh Le, Tuan Vu Ho, Dung Tran, and Duc Thanh Chau. Paper affiliations include ZaloAI, independent research, and Vietnam National University/Ho Chi Minh City University of Science.
- **Exact documentation title:** “ChunkFormer: Masked Chunking Conformer For Long-Form Speech Transcription”
- **Official documentation URL:** https://github.com/khanld/chunkformer
- **Publication/access:** ICASSP 2025; arXiv submission February 20, 2025; README update date not established; accessed October 10, 2026. **The verified bibliographic year is 2025, not 2024.** ([huggingface.co](https://huggingface.co/khanhld/chunkformer-ctc-large-vie/resolve/main/paper.pdf?download=true&utm_source=openai))

**Verbatim official-documentation quote:**

> “ChunkFormer is an ASR model designed for processing long audio inputs effectively on low-memory GPUs.” ([github.com](https://github.com/khanld/chunkformer))

The README describes chunk-wise processing with relative right context and a Masked Batch technique to reduce padding-related memory waste. Its documented focus is efficient long-form processing; this is **not evidence that every transcript is verbatim-correct or hallucination-free**. ([github.com](https://github.com/khanld/chunkformer))

## 3. Whisper hallucinations and low-resource weaknesses

### 3A. Koenecke et al. — measured hallucination percentage

- **Owner/organisation:** Research authors Allison Koenecke, Anna Seo Gyeong Choi, Katelyn X. Mei, Hilke Schellmann, and Mona Sloane; published by ACM; retrieved copy hosted by TalkBank.
- **Exact paper title:** “Careless Whisper: Speech-to-Text Hallucination Harms”
- **URL:** https://talkbank.org/aphasia/publications/2024/Koenecke24.pdf
- **Publication/access:** FAccT 2024, June 3–6, 2024; accessed October 10, 2026.
- **Official product/help page documenting this study’s percentage:** **NOT FOUND.** The percentage is a research finding, not an OpenAI product specification. ([talkbank.org](https://talkbank.org/aphasia/publications/2024/Koenecke24.pdf))

**Verbatim primary-paper excerpt:**

> “1.4% of audio segments yielded a hallucination sequence in Whisper transcriptions” ([talkbank.org](https://talkbank.org/aphasia/publications/2024/Koenecke24.pdf))

**Scope:** The researchers processed 13,140 English AphasiaBank audio segments and manually confirmed 187 hallucination-producing segments. The quoted 1.4% pertains to experiments in **April and May 2023**, including speakers with aphasia and controls. It is **not** a universal rate, a Vietnamese rate, or a current Whisper-large-v3/turbo rate. ([talkbank.org](https://talkbank.org/aphasia/publications/2024/Koenecke24.pdf))

> “LibriSpeech Clean 2.7 2.7 0.0”

## Defensible conclusion

My synthesis: **“Treat ASR output as a draft, not as a verified verbatim record. Check consequential wording against the original audio.”** That follows from the official hallucination warning, measured fabricated sequences, nonzero Vietnamese benchmark WER, and substantial cross-domain error variation. It does **not** mean every transcript is wrong, or that the historical 1.4% rate applies today. ([raw.githubusercontent.com](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/whisper/main/model-card.md))

The search established the named papers, current author/vendor documentation, and general domain-shift evidence. It did not establish an unmodified-Whisper comparison in PhoWhisper’s paper, a current numerical hallucination rate, or the exact controlled music-overlaid Vietnamese short-video comparison.
